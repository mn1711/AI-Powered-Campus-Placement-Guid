Phase 1: Amazon Hackon
The first step in the recruitment process was the Amazon Hackon. Successfully navigating this phase is crucial for receiving the hiring interest form.
● Format: 2 Data Structures and Algorithms (DSA) questions and 1 SQL question. There will be 20 - 25 MCQs based on DSA and CS Fundamentals as well.
● Difficulty: The DSA questions typically consist of one medium and one easy problem. During my attempt, the medium question was based on bit manipulation.
● Outcome: I was selected up to the prototype round. Solving all three questions generally prompts Amazon to share their hiring interest form.

Phase 2: Online Assessment (OA)
Following the hiring interest form, candidates are invited to complete the Online Assessment.
● Technical Section: Comprised 2 DSA questions. One was mathematical, and the other was similar to the Number of Visible People in a Queue problem.
● Behavioral Section: An approximately 1-hour behavioral assessment focused heavily on Amazon's Leadership Principles (LPs).
● Pro Tip: The behavioral assessment is critical. Ensure your answers are consistent and do not contradict each other. Amazon takes this very seriously.

Phase 3: Virtual Interview
The technical interview was conducted on Amazon Chime, and code was written on an internal Amazon document rather than a standard IDE.
● Interviewer: Software Development Engineer II (SDE 2) from NSUT Delhi.
● Question 1: Longest Substring Without Repeating Characters. The interviewer expected both the standard sliding window approach and its optimized version.
● Question 2: Maximum Area in a Histogram (Largest Rectangle in Histogram).
● CS Fundamentals: Core subjects may or may not be asked depending on the flow of the interview. During my interview, I was specifically asked about Distributed Systems.
● Behavioral/Project Discussion: While some peers were asked about their projects, my discussion was entirely centered around Leadership Principles.

Key Takeaways
● Solve Two Problems: You must solve 2 questions within the allotted interview time. If you only solve one and the interviewer cannot reach the second question, it is a strong minus.
● Pacing: Try to solve the easier question within the first 15 minutes. This will give you 25-30 minutes to confidently tackle the second question.
● Verbal Communication: Practice explaining your thought process verbally without relying on pen and paper. Strong communication skills are vital.
● Leadership Principles (LPs): Amazon takes its LPs very seriously. Thoroughly read up on principles like Bias for Action, Ownership, and Deep Dive before your interview.
● Resume Alignment: Make sure to reflect these Leadership Principles through your projects and work experience on your resume.
● Coding Environment: Get accustomed to writing code in a plain document environment without syntax highlighting or auto-complete.
