Moneyview Interview Experience (On-Campus Placement)
Company Overview
Company: Moneyview (FinTech)
Moneyview conducted a parallel pool recruitment drive across four NITs:
• NIT Jamshedpur
• NIT Rourkela
• NIT Trichy
• NIT Raipur

Eligibility Criteria
• Minimum CPI: 8.0
• Eligible Branches for Online Assessment: All branches
• Eligible Branches for Interview Rounds: CSE, IT, and ECE

Round 1: Online Assessment (OA)
The Online Assessment consisted of approximately 60 MCQs along with 2 DSA coding questions.
Coding Questions
• The first question was based on the Fibonacci series, but it was not the standard implementation. It required a slightly different approach.
• The second question was related to DFS (Depth First Search). I initially attempted it using DSU (Disjoint Set Union), although the expected approach was based on graph traversal.

The overall OA was of medium difficulty, with a balanced mix of aptitude, computer science fundamentals, and coding.
I was not able to solve 2nd completely some test cases were failing ,but the main game was of Aptitude , so around 30-32 people were selected for interview

Technical Interview Round 1 (Approximately 1 hour 15 minutes)
This round mainly focused on Data Structures & Algorithms along with Computer Science Fundamentals.

DSA Questions
I was asked to solve three coding problems:
• Binary Search on a Rotated Sorted Array
• A question based on Binary Trees
• Detect Loop in a Linked List

The interviewer was equally interested in understanding my thought process rather than just the final code, well I was able to solve all the questions , make it interactive by telling the approach and discuss it while you code each and every line why you wrote it 

OOPs & C++
Since my primary programming language was C++, the interviewer shifted towards Object-Oriented Programming concepts.
Some of the topics discussed were:
• OOP Principles
• Operator Overloading (along with coding)
• C++ concepts and implementation-based questions

DBMS
The interviewer also asked questions related to:
• Database Sharding
• Memory Management Techniques
• Basic DBMS concepts

Overall, this round was a good combination of DSA + CS Fundamentals.

Technical Interview Round 2 (Approximately 1 hour)
Personally, I found this round to be more challenging than the first one.
The interviewer focused heavily on System Design and object-oriented design concepts.

Topics Covered
• SOLID Principles
• Real-life examples based on SOLID principles
• Basic Object-Oriented Design

System Design Question
I was asked to design a Chess Game.
I wasn't completely familiar with designing the entire system. However, instead of giving up, I started by explaining my approach.
I created:
• Classes
• Data Members
• Member Functions
• A basic UML Diagram

The interviewer was not expecting a complete implementation. Instead, he appreciated the way I approached the problem and how I structured my design.
One important takeaway from this interview was:
Even if you don't know the complete solution, explain your thought process confidently. Interviewers often value your approach more than a perfect answer.

Operating System & Computer Networks
The discussion then moved towards OS and CN.
Some of the questions included:
• Basic Operating System concepts
• Computer Networks fundamentals
• A real-world example involving a Firewall
• How many cores are present in a computer?
• General system-related questions

Fortunately, I had prepared Computer Networks well, which really helped me during this round.
One interesting thing the interviewer mentioned was that the previous candidate wasn't comfortable answering Computer Networks questions, so having a strong foundation definitely made a positive impression.

HR Interview
The HR round was quite relaxed.
The HR mentioned that he was happy with my performance in the technical rounds as said by the past interviewers.
Some of the questions asked were:
• Are you comfortable relocating to Bangalore?
• Tell me about yourself.
• What are your hobbies?
• How was your interview experience?
• General discussion about my interests and career goals.

Overall, it was more of a conversation aimed at understanding my personality and ensuring that I was a good cultural fit for the company.

Some Useful Tips
1. Be mindful of what you speak.
Always think before answering.
Keep your answers short, crisp, and to the point. Interviewers generally appreciate clarity more than lengthy explanations.

2. Take a few seconds before answering.
If you're asked a coding or conceptual question, it's completely okay to take 30–60 seconds to think.
A well-structured answer is much better than rushing into an incorrect one.

3. Communicate while solving coding questions.
One mistake many candidates make is solving the question silently.
Instead:
• Start with the brute-force approach.
• Explain why it isn't optimal.
• Gradually move towards the optimized solution.
• Keep explaining your thought process while writing code.
This creates a healthy interaction with the interviewer and shows that you genuinely understand what you're doing instead of reproducing a memorized solution.

4. Even if you don't know the complete answer, don't stop.
For design questions especially, explain whatever you know.
Draw diagrams, define classes, discuss possible approaches, and keep the conversation going.
Interviewers usually appreciate candidates who can think logically under pressure.

5. Revise CS Fundamentals thoroughly.
Apart from DSA, make sure you're comfortable with:
• Object-Oriented Programming
• DBMS
• Operating Systems
• Computer Networks
• Basic System Design
These subjects were discussed extensively during my interviews.

Final Thoughts
Overall, the interview experience was a great learning opportunity.
The interviewers were friendly and mostly tried to understand how I think and approach problems, rather than expecting perfect answers to every question.
My advice would be to stay calm, communicate your thought process clearly, and never hesitate to attempt a question even if you don't know the complete solution.
